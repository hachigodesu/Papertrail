# M11.5 results

Tested on 8 November 2026 on Windows, Arch and Ubuntu (WSL2) on the development PC. Nothing has been tried by hand yet.

M11.5 adds the features you chose before the release stage: daily backups, removing publications, dragging to collections and tags with nested collections, searching highlights and notes, retraction warnings, finding an issue's articles without a table of contents, annotated PDF copies, and summaries in batches at half price.

## Backups

- **Daily:** about 10 seconds after the library opens, a copy is made in the background if there isn't one from today. The last 7 are kept. It can be turned off in **File > Backups...**.
- **Before changes:** a copy is made before a newer Papertrail upgrades the database (and the library isn't opened if that copy can't be made), before an import replaces the library, and before a backup is restored. The last 3 of each kind are kept.
- **File > Backups...** lists every backup with when and why it was made, and can make one by hand (never removed automatically), restore one, or show the folder.
- **Restoring** replaces the open library's contents with the backup's, page by page through SQLite, with everything else in the window stopped first. The library as it was is backed up first, so a restore can be undone. A backup from an older version is brought up to date; one from a newer version, a damaged one, or something that isn't a library is refused without changing anything.
- Backups are kept in a `backups` folder beside the library. Replace's backup from M11 moved there too.

## Removing publications

- **Remove from library...** in the right-click menu, or the **Delete** key in the grid or list, asks first, then deletes the details, tags, notes, highlights and any articles inside. The files themselves are never touched.
- **Their files stay out:** the files are remembered by their contents, so a rescan, or the same file in another folder, doesn't bring them back. A file that couldn't be read, or an empty one, is remembered by itself instead, since its contents say nothing.
- **Library folders** says how many are kept out, with **Bring back...** to list them and choose any to add back at the next scan, as new publications.
- **Exports** include what was removed, by contents, so it stays out on another computer too. A library that still has the same paper keeps it: merging never takes anything away.

## Dragging, and nested collections

- **Dragging:** pressing on a chosen cover or row and dragging carries everything chosen. Dropping it on a collection or tag in the sidebar adds them, through the same background changes the menus use. Pressing on anything not chosen still chooses by dragging, as fixed earlier.
- **Nested collections:** **New collection inside this one...** and **Move...** in a collection's right-click menu. Choosing a collection shows what's in it and in the collections inside it, and its count includes them, each counted once. Deleting one deletes those inside it too, after saying so. Names stay unique across the library, so menus show each as its path, such as "Thesis > Chapter 2".
- **Articles follow their issue:** adding an issue to a collection adds its articles, and taking it out takes them out. Articles made later join their issue's collections. Libraries opened with this version have their existing issues' articles added to the same collections.
- Exports say which collection each is inside.

## Searching highlights and notes

- **`note:welds`** searches only highlighted words, notes on pages, and each publication's own notes. Phrases work, as in `note:"along the welds"`.
- **Plain words** find these too, alongside the details. A field such as `author:` still has to match as well.
- **View > Highlights and notes...** (Ctrl+Shift+N) lists every highlight and note in the library, newest first, with what it's in and its page. It can be searched, and opening one goes to its place in its document. The newest 2,000 are listed, with a search to find others.
- `note:` is listed in the search box's **?** menu.

## Retraction warnings

- **What's checked:** with online lookups on, each publication's DOI is sent to Crossref about once a month, 20 to a request, to find retractions, corrections, expressions of concern and the like published since. Crossref includes Retraction Watch's data. Checking starts a minute after opening and then every six hours, after any lookups, and only asks about what's due.
- **What's sent:** only DOIs, and the contact email if you entered one. Nothing in offline mode, before a mode is chosen, or with checking turned off under **File > Online lookups...**.
- **What's shown:** a retraction, withdrawal or expression of concern is said plainly near the top of the details panel, such as "This was retracted in 2010.", and every notice is listed with the publication's details, with a link to it. **Retracted or of concern** in the filters lists them. A message says when a check finds new ones.

## Articles without a table of contents

**Create articles from contents...** now works on an issue with no table of contents. Every page is read for its largest text and its DOIs, in the background, and:

- a page with a DOI that isn't the current article's starts an article, titled from its largest text
- the article runs until the next one starts, or until one of the issue's own pages such as "Information for Authors"
- pages before the first article are the issue's
- only DOIs from the publisher most of the issue's DOIs come from count, so a cited paper's DOI on a later page doesn't start an article
- a page like "Table of Contents" that has a DOI is offered but not chosen

At least two articles have to be found, since one alone is most likely a single paper. Everything found is shown for checking, as with contents, and nothing is made until you choose to. Files over 2,000 pages aren't read this way.

## Annotated PDF copies

**Save a copy with highlights...** in a publication's right-click menu writes a new PDF with its highlights, underlines and notes added as ordinary PDF annotations, which any PDF reader shows. It's offered for PDFs with marks on their pages, including those made in an issue's articles. The copy is written by the worker from a freshly opened copy of the document. The original is never written, and a path naming it is refused. The suggested place is your Documents folder, since a copy in one of the library's folders would be added as a publication of its own.

## Summaries at half price

- **Summarize later at half price...** is in the right-click menu for one publication, beside **Summarize with Claude...**, and for several. It's always offered, as you chose.
- **Confirming:** the dialog says what's sent, which is only what identifies each publication and never the paper, your notes or tags. It lets you choose the model and effort, and gives an estimate at half price worked out on this computer. Publications that already have a summary are left out unless you choose to replace them. At most 500 go in one batch.
- **Collecting:** results are checked every three minutes while any are on their way, and when the library opens. One that paused partway, as web searches sometimes do, is carried on in another batch, up to five times. One that fails says why.
- **On the publication:** its details say a summary is on its way, has arrived, or failed, with a button to the list. The status bar says how many are on their way or waiting to be reviewed, and opens the list too.
- **View > Summaries from Claude...** lists what's on its way, with when it was sent and its model, and any that failed with the reason, which can be taken off the list. Those that have arrived wait under **To review**, showing the summary, its sources, what it's based on and what it cost. **Keep** makes it the publication's summary, replacing any it has, and **Discard** drops it after asking. **Keep all** keeps every one.
- **Nothing is lost by closing Papertrail:** a batch on its way is asked about next time, everything a batch brings back is noted in the library in one go, what arrived waits to be reviewed however often Papertrail closes, and what paused is noted before it's sent on, so it's sent the next time if it couldn't be then. Anthropic keeps results for 29 days, so they must be collected within that.
- **Spending:** the list's **Spending** page gives this month's, the last 30 days' and all spending, split into straight away and half price, and lists every reply with what it was for, its model and cost. Each reply is counted when it comes back, whether or not its summary is kept, including instant summaries that were discarded. It's worked out on this computer from Anthropic's token counts and the prices last checked, so the Claude Console has the exact bill. Wherever the app names the Console, it links to it: its billing page from here, and its API keys page where a key is asked for. Spending from before this list existed only includes summaries that were kept.
- The key is read from the system's store each time, and only goes in requests to Anthropic. The results' address is built from Anthropic's own, never taken from a reply.

## Decisions I made

- **Batches never send the paper.** Sending one needs its own confirmation, which doesn't fit a batch. A summary that couldn't find the paper says so, and can be redone straight away with the paper.
- **Retraction checks are on by default** when lookups are online, since they only send DOIs, which lookups already send.
- **Removed publications are remembered by contents, not path,** as you chose, so the same paper downloaded again with the same bytes stays out.
- **Collection names stay unique across the library,** rather than among siblings, so a name always means one collection in a menu.
- **The daily backup doesn't include the full-text index,** which is rebuilt from your files when needed and can be large.

## How it differs from the plan

- **Nesting** is kept in a table of its own (`collection_parent`) rather than a column on collections, because migrations here must be safe to run again, and adding a column can't be.
- **Notices aren't included in exports.** The other computer checks for itself.
- **Batches waiting aren't exported either.** Their summaries arrive in the library that sent them. Nor is spending, which is this computer's record.
- **Batch summaries wait to be reviewed** rather than being kept as they arrive, after hand testing found there was no way to see them coming or check them. Batch items moved to a new table (`summary_request`) for the new states, since a check can't be changed in place.

## Problems testing found

- **Dragging to select** worked in tests that called the selection code directly but not with the mouse: Qt's grid only followed the pointer over a cover. Turning on its selection rectangle fixed it, and the test now drags with real mouse events.
- **A test assumed Windows' grid layout,** with four covers across where Arch has three.
- **Migrations are run again by tests** that rewind a library's version, so a migration adding a column failed. Nesting moved to its own table.
- **Two backups made in the same second** were listed in the wrong order. They're now told apart by when their files were written.
- **Retraction checks looped** when told to recheck everything at once, since what a run had checked fell due again. A run now checks each publication once at most.
- **Hand testing found** nothing on a publication said a summary was on its way, there was nowhere to see what was waiting, and no way to see spending. All three were added, and summaries now wait to be reviewed.
- **Pages starting an article** were missed when they also cited another paper, since the worker only reported a page's DOI if it had just one. It now lists them all, and the first from the issue's publisher counts.

## How it's been tested

- **Automated:** all test programs pass on all three platforms, with new tests for each feature:
  - backups: making, naming, keeping the newest, restoring with another connection open, undoing a restore, older and unusable backups, the upgrade backup, the daily backup and the dialog
  - removing: by the window and Delete key, rescans, another copy elsewhere, unreadable files, bringing back, and exports, with the documents' hashes checked before and after
  - dragging with real mouse and drop events, nested collections in queries, counts and the sidebar, and articles following their issue
  - `note:` and plain searches, with hostile input, edits and deletions, and the notes list opening a highlight's page
  - Crossref's notices read from made-up replies, batching that sends only DOIs, rechecking, failures, the details panel, and that nothing is sent offline or with checking off, through the real network code
  - finding articles from made-up pages, and from a new generated sample with no contents
  - a marked copy with a highlight, an underline and a note, the original's hash unchanged, and refusing to overwrite it
  - batches sent, not yet finished, collected at half price, carried on after a pause, failing with a reason, refused keys, and the window collecting one, all with a fake transport and no real API calls
  - closing Papertrail between sending, collecting and carrying on, a refused continuation, reviewing in the window, spending added up by month and kind, and a library from before this change keeping its batch
- **Not tested yet:**
  - **By hand,** including every new dialog's wording and layout.
  - **A real Crossref check** from the app, though its replies were checked against two well-known public retractions while building it.
  - **A real batch.** Its format follows Anthropic's documentation, but no real request was made, as the rules say.
  - **The copy in other PDF readers,** such as Acrobat or a browser.
  - **Restoring a large library,** for how long it takes.

## Known limits

- **Restoring closes open documents,** and the library can't be used while it happens, as with importing.
- **Retractions are only found for publications with a DOI.**
- **Without contents, articles are found only where each starts with a DOI.** An issue whose articles have none still needs them added by hand.
- **A marked copy only has marks from fixed pages.** EPUB and other reflowable formats have no pages to put them on, and only PDFs can be written.
- **Batch summaries only arrive while Papertrail is open.** Anthropic keeps results for 29 days, after which anything uncollected is lost and said to have failed.
